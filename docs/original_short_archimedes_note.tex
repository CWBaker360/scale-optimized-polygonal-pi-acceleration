\section*{Classical Setting}

For an inscribed regular $n$-gon:
\[
E_n = 2\pi - P_n \sim C n^{-2}.
\]

Thus doubling gives:
\[
E_{2n} \approx \tfrac{1}{4} E_n.
\]

\section*{Standard Scaling}

Replacing $n \to kn$ yields:
\[
E_{kn} \approx \tfrac{1}{k^2} E_n.
\]

This follows directly from the asymptotic law.

\section*{Nontrivial Question}

Is $k>2$ refinement realizable constructibly within a geometric framework?

\section*{Main Structural Claim}

A constructible subdivision mechanism exists that realizes
systematic $k$-fold refinement (specifically $k=3$) with controlled error.

\[
6 \;\to\; 18 \;\to\; 54 \;\to\; 162 \;\to\; \cdots
\]

\[
E_{3n} \approx \tfrac{1}{9} E_n.
\]

\section*{Algebraic Perspective}

Let $c = \cos\theta$ and consider:
\[
T_n(y) = c.
\]

\begin{itemize}
\item Exact solution: $y = \cos(\theta/n)$ lies in a non-quadratic algebraic extension
\item Constructible approximation: $Y_n(c)$ lies in the quadratic closure of $\mathbb{Q}(c)$
\end{itemize}

Thus:
\begin{itemize}
\item Exact subdivision requires solving $T_n(y) - c = 0$
\item Approximate subdivision remains constructible
\end{itemize}
